# Process

## Starting a design
- **Start with a feature, not a layout.** Don't design the shell (nav, container, logo placement) first — you don't have the info yet. Design a real piece of functionality, then let the shell emerge.
- **Design in low fidelity first.** Sketches/wireframes are disposable. Use a thick Sharpie or grayscale to avoid getting trapped in details.
- **Design in grayscale before adding color.** Forces spacing, contrast, and size to do the hierarchy work. Add color last.
- **Don't design every feature upfront.** Work in short cycles: design simple → build → iterate on the working thing.
- **Be a pessimist.** Don't imply functionality you aren't ready to build. Design the smallest shippable version; treat nice-to-haves as later.

## Personality
A design's personality is driven by concrete factors, not vibes:
- **Font choice** — serif = elegant/classic, rounded sans = playful, neutral sans = plain.
- **Color** — blue = safe, gold = expensive, pink = fun. Pick by feel, not psychology theory.
- **Border radius** — small = neutral, large = playful, none = serious/formal. Stay consistent — don't mix.
- **Language** — formal vs. casual wording shifts perceived personality as much as visuals.

When unsure of personality, look at sites your audience already uses. Don't copy direct competitors.

## Constraints
- **Limit your choices.** Unlimited options = decision paralysis. Define systems in advance (font sizes, colors, spacing, shadows, radii, weights, etc.).
- **Design by process of elimination.** With a constrained scale, pick a likely value, compare to neighbors, eliminate.
- **Systematize everything** that you might decide twice: font size, weight, line-height, color, margin, padding, width, height, box shadow, border radius, border width, opacity.
